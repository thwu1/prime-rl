DELTA RATIO (Delta-Delta)
=========================

Delta Ratio = (increase in Anion Gap) / (decrease in HCO3-)

Using standard reference values:

  Delta Ratio = (AG – 12) / (24 – HCO3-)

The delta ratio quantifies the relationship between the change in anion gap and
the change in bicarbonate from their normal reference values. If one molecule of
metabolic acid (HA) is added to the ECF and dissociates, the one H+ released
reacts with one molecule of HCO3-. The net effect is an increase in unmeasured
anions by one (anion gap increases by one) and a decrease in bicarbonate by one.

WHEN TO USE
-----------
Check the delta ratio in the presence of a high anion gap metabolic acidosis
(HAGMA) to determine if it is a 'pure' HAGMA or if there is coexistent NAGMA
or metabolic alkalosis.

ASSUMPTIONS ABOUT BUFFERING
----------------------------
50% of buffering for a metabolic acidosis occurs intracellularly. The acid anion
tends to stay extracellularly so the increase in anion gap will tend to be as
much as predicted, but HCO3- will not fall as far. This causes the delta ratio
to be greater than one in a pure HAGMA.

INTERPRETATION
--------------
< 0.4
  Hyperchloraemic normal anion gap acidosis (pure NAGMA). The acid is
  effectively HCl; the rise in chloride is accounted for in the AG calculation.

0.4 – 0.8
  Consider combined HAGMA + NAGMA. Note the ratio is often < 1 in acidosis
  associated with renal failure.

0.8 – 2.0
  Usual for uncomplicated HAGMA. Lactic acidosis: average ratio 1.6. DKA more
  likely to have a ratio closer to 1 due to urinary ketone loss.

> 2.0
  A high delta ratio can occur when the patient had quite an elevated
  bicarbonate at the onset of the metabolic acidosis — due to pre-existing
  metabolic alkalosis, or compensation for a pre-existing chronic respiratory
  acidosis.

SOURCES OF ERROR
----------------
Calculation requires measurement of 4 electrolytes, each with a measurement
error. Changes are assessed against standard normal values for both anion gap
(12) and bicarbonate (24).
