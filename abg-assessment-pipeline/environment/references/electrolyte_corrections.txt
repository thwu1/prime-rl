ELECTROLYTE CORRECTIONS IN ACID-BASE DISTURBANCES
===================================================

SODIUM CORRECTION IN HYPERGLYCAEMIA
------------------------------------
In patients with marked hyperglycaemia, the elevated serum glucose raises the
serum tonicity (as glucose cannot enter cells), which pulls water out of cells
and expands the extracellular water compartment — thereby lowering the
concentration of sodium.

The corrected serum sodium represents what the serum sodium concentration would
be if the glucose level was reduced back to normal. If the corrected sodium is
in a normal range, the patient does not have a concurrent hypotonic
hyponatraemia.

  [Na]corrected = [Na] + 1.5 × ([Glucose – 5.5] / 5.5)

Rough approximation: [Na]corrected ≈ [Na] + (Glucose – 5) / 3

(Glucose in mmol/L, Na in mmol/L)

POTASSIUM CORRECTION WITH pH ABNORMALITIES
-------------------------------------------
Acid-base disturbances affect the serum concentration of potassium. Acidaemia
increases the measured serum potassium by causing extracellular shift, and
alkalaemia reduces the measured serum potassium by causing intracellular shift.

The described relationship is that for every 0.1 unit change in pH, there will
be a 0.6 mEq/L change in serum potassium.

  K+corrected = [K+]measured – 0.6 × ([7.4 – pH] / 0.1)

This estimates what the potassium would be at a normal pH of 7.4. In acidaemia
(pH < 7.4), the measured K+ is higher than the "true" K+ due to extracellular
shift, so the correction subtracts from measured K+. In alkalaemia (pH > 7.4),
the formula adds to measured K+ since intracellular shift artificially lowers
the measured value.

Source: LITFL Critical Care Compendium — Blood Gas Interpretation
(Jeremy Rogers, Mike Cadogan)
