STRONG ION DIFFERENCE AND STEWART'S PHYSICOCHEMICAL APPROACH
=============================================================

The quantitative (physicochemical or Stewart) approach to acid-base chemistry
was proposed by Canadian physiologist Peter Stewart in 1981. It provides a
mathematical explanation of the relevant variables that control H+ in body
fluids and their interactions.

The Stewart approach shows that pH is not simply determined by [H+] and [HCO3-]
(as in Henderson-Hasselbalch) but involves interactions of three independent
variables: pCO2, ATOT (total weak non-volatile acids), and SID (net Strong Ion
Difference).

STRONG ION DIFFERENCE (SID)
---------------------------
Strong ions dissociate completely at physiological pH. In blood at pH 7.4:
  Strong cations: Na+, K+, Ca2+, Mg2+
  Strong anions: Cl-, SO42-

SID = [strong cations] – [strong anions]

Apparent SID (SIDa):
  SIDa = (Na+ + K+ + Ca2+ + Mg2+) – (Cl- + L-lactate)

Note: Ca2+ and Mg2+ are divalent ions. When concentrations are given in mmol/L,
multiply by their valence (2) to convert to mEq/L for the SID calculation.

Normal SIDa ≈ 42 mEq/L

Effective SID (SIDe):
  SIDe = [HCO3-] + [A-]

where A- is the net negative charge contribution of weak acids (primarily
albumin). The albumin charge varies with pH.

The Figge-Fencl equation provides a linear approximation for the albumin
contribution to A-:
  A- ≈ [albumin](g/L) × f(pH)

where f is a linear function of pH. This relationship can be derived
empirically or found in Stewart acid-base physiology literature.

STRONG ION GAP (SIG)
--------------------
  SIG = SIDa – SIDe

SIG is analogous to the albumin-corrected anion gap but has the advantage of
fewer unmeasured components. SIG is independent of pH, albumin, PO4, Ca, Mg.

High SIG → increased unmeasured anions
Low SIG → increased unmeasured cations

ATOT
----
ATOT = total plasma concentration of inorganic phosphate, serum proteins, and
albumin (weak non-volatile acids).
  ATOT = [PiTOT] + [PrTOT] + [albumin]
  Hypoproteinaemia = base excess
