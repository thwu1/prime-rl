OSMOLAR GAP
===========

An osmole is the amount of a substance that yields the number of particles that
would depress the freezing point of the solvent by 1.86K. Osmolality is measured
in the laboratory by osmometers. Only osmometers using the freezing point
depression method can detect volatile alcohols which can abnormally increase the
osmolar gap.

CALCULATION
-----------
  Calculated osmolarity = (2 × [Na+]) + [glucose] + [urea]

All values must be in mmol/L.

  Osmolar gap = Osmolality(measured) – Osmolarity(calculated)
  Normal osmolar gap < 10

Note: this is a pragmatic clinical aid — the units are different (osmolality =
mOsm/kg and osmolarity = mOsm/L) so it does not make strict mathematical
sense.

ACCOUNTING FOR ETHANOL
----------------------
If ethanol levels are measured they can be added to the calculated osmolarity
to exclude ethanol as a contributor to the osmolar gap:

  Calculated osmolarity = (2 × [Na+]) + [glucose] + [urea] + (1.25 × EtOH)

where EtOH must be in mmol/L. To convert:

  EtOH (mmol/L) = EtOH (mg/dL) ÷ 4.6

MEANING OF A HIGH OSMOLAR GAP
------------------------------
The presence of an abnormal solute in significant amounts. The solute must have
a low molecular weight and be uncharged to elevate the osmolar gap.

CAUSES OF HIGH OSMOLAR GAP
---------------------------
If elevated, consider the presence of other osmotically active particles:
mannitol, methanol, ethylene glycol, sorbitol, polyethylene glycol (IV
lorazepam), propylene glycol (IV lorazepam, diazepam, phenytoin), glycine
(TURP syndrome), maltose (IV immunoglobulin).
