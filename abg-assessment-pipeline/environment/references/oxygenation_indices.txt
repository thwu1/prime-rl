OXYGENATION INDICES
====================

PaO2/FiO2 RATIO (P/F Ratio, Horowitz Index)
--------------------------------------------
The P/F ratio is the ratio of arterial oxygen partial pressure (PaO2 in mmHg)
to fractional inspired oxygen (FiO2 expressed as a fraction, not a percentage).

  P/F ratio = PaO2 / FiO2

At sea level, the normal PaO2/FiO2 ratio is approximately 400-500 mmHg.

Quick rule of thumb: PaO2 should ≈ FiO2 × 500

ARDS Berlin Definition (2012):
  Mild:     P/F 200 – 300 mmHg    (27% mortality)
  Moderate: P/F 100 – 200 mmHg    (32% mortality)
  Severe:   P/F < 100 mmHg        (45% mortality)

Note: the Berlin definition ratios apply with a PEEP setting of ≥ 5 cmH2O.

Limitations: P/F ratio is dependent on barometric pressure, does not
distinguish hypoventilation from V/Q mismatch, and is markedly dependent on
FiO2. It may be unreliable unless FiO2 > 0.5 and PaO2 < 100 mmHg.

OXYGEN EXTRACTION RATIO (O2ER)
-------------------------------
O2ER = VO2 / DO2 = (SaO2 – SvO2) / SaO2

where SaO2 is arterial oxygen saturation and SvO2 is mixed venous oxygen
saturation. Normal O2ER is approximately 25%.

Under resting conditions with normal distribution of cardiac output, DO2 is
more than adequate to meet VO2. Oxygen that is not extracted returns to the
mixed venous circulation. ScvO2 of 70% indicates oxygen delivery is adequate.

O2ER varies by organ: cardiac O2ER >60%, hepatic 45-55%, renal <15%.

At critical DO2 (cDO2), maximum O2ER is reached (approximately 70%). Beyond
this, any further increase in VO2 or decline in DO2 leads to tissue hypoxia
and anaerobic metabolism.

High O2ER suggests inadequate oxygen delivery (shock, hypoxia, anaemia) or
increased oxygen consumption (fever, sepsis, increased muscular activity).

Low O2ER suggests increased oxygen delivery (hyperoxia, ECMO) or decreased
oxygen consumption (hypothermia, sedation, paralysis, histotoxic hypoxia).

OXYGENATION INDEX (OI)
-----------------------
  OI = (FiO2 × MAP) / PaO2

where MAP is mean airway pressure. OI may be a more accurate measure of
oxygenation dysfunction in ventilated patients as it accounts for MAP.

Source: LITFL Critical Care Compendium — PaO2/FiO2 Ratio, Oxygen Extraction
Ratio (Chris Nickson)
