REGULATION Z, APPENDIX J — APR COMPUTATION SPECIFICATION
(Condensed from 12 CFR Part 1026, Appendix J — Actuarial Method)

=========================================================================
1. OVERVIEW
=========================================================================
The actuarial method computes the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) by finding
the unit-period rate i such that the present value of all advances equals
the present value of all payments. The APR (nominal annual rate) is:

    APR = i * w

where w = number of unit-periods per year.

Under the actuarial method, at the end of each unit-period (or fractional
unit-period) the unpaid balance is increased by the finance charge earned
during that period and decreased by the total payment made. Unpaid finance
charges are capitalized (interest computed on interest).

=========================================================================
2. TERM OF THE TRANSACTION
=========================================================================
The term begins on the date of consummation (or later date if the finance
charge begins accruing later). It ends on the date the last payment is due
(or later if an advance is scheduled after that date).

=========================================================================
3. DEFINITIONS OF TIME INTERVALS
=========================================================================
- Period: interval between consecutive advances or consecutive payments,
  including the interval from consummation to the first advance or first
  payment.
- Common period: any period that occurs more than once in a transaction.
- Standard interval: day, week, semimonth, month, or a multiple of a week
  or month up to (but not exceeding) 1 year.
- Month convention: ALL MONTHS ARE CONSIDERED EQUAL. Full months are
  measured from a given date in one month to the same date in another
  month. If payments are scheduled for the 29th or 30th, use the last day
  of February when applicable. If a series is scheduled for the last day
  of each month, measure from last day to last day.

=========================================================================
4. UNIT-PERIOD DETERMINATION
=========================================================================
(a) For all transactions EXCEPT single-advance, single-payment:
    The unit-period is the common period (not exceeding 1 year) that
    occurs MOST FREQUENTLY in the transaction.
    - Tie: if 2+ common periods occur equally often, use the SMALLER.
    - No common period: use the average of all periods, rounded to the
      nearest whole standard interval. If equally near two standard
      intervals, use the lower.

(b) For single-advance, single-payment transactions:
    Unit-period = term of the transaction, but not exceeding 1 year.

=========================================================================
5. NUMBER OF UNIT-PERIODS BETWEEN TWO DATES
=========================================================================
The number of days between two dates is the number of 24-hour intervals
between the same point in time on each date.

(a) MONTHLY unit-period:
    - Full unit-periods = number of months measured BACK from the LATER
      date.
    - Fractional part = number of days measured FORWARD from the EARLIER
      date to the beginning of the first full unit-period, divided by 30.
    - Unit-periods per year (w) = 12.

(b) SEMIMONTH or MULTI-MONTH (2..11 months) unit-period:
    - Total "days" = 30 * full_months_back + remaining_actual_days.
    - Divide total by 15 (semimonth) or by (months_per_period * 30).
    - w = 24 (semimonth) or 12/months_per_period (multi-month).

(c) DAY, WEEK, or MULTI-WEEK unit-period:
    - Divide actual days between dates by days_per_unit_period.
    - w = 365 (day), or 52/weeks_per_period (week/multi-week).

(d) YEAR unit-period:
    - Full years (12 months each) back from later date.
    - Remainder: months/12 if whole months remain, else days/365.

(e) SINGLE-ADVANCE, SINGLE-PAYMENT, term < 1 year, WHOLE MONTHS:
    - 1 unit-period in term.
    - w = 12 / months_in_term.

(f) SINGLE-ADVANCE, SINGLE-PAYMENT, term < 1 year, NOT whole months:
    - 1 unit-period in term.
    - w = 365 / days_in_term.

=========================================================================
6. FRACTIONAL PERIOD RATE
=========================================================================
The finance charge rate for a fraction f (where 0 <= f < 1) of a
unit-period equals f * i (simple interest — NOT compound).

=========================================================================
7. GENERAL EQUATION
=========================================================================
The present value of all advances must equal the present value of all
payments:

  SUM_j [ A_j / ((1 + f_j * i) * (1 + i)^n_j) ]
  =
  SUM_k [ P_k / ((1 + g_k * i) * (1 + i)^m_k) ]

where:
  A_j = amount of the j-th advance
  P_k = amount of the k-th payment
  i   = unit-period rate (the unknown)
  n_j = integer number of full unit-periods from term start to advance j
  f_j = fractional unit-period for advance j (0 <= f_j < 1)
  m_k = integer number of full unit-periods from term start to payment k
  g_k = fractional unit-period for payment k (0 <= g_k < 1)

The discount factor for a cash flow at time = n + f unit-periods is:

  DF(n, f, i) = 1 / ((1 + f*i) * (1+i)^n)

Note: when n=0 and f=0, DF = 1 (no discounting at the zero point).

=========================================================================
8. SOLVING BY ITERATION (Newton-Raphson)
=========================================================================
Define F(i) = SUM[A_j * DF(n_j, f_j, i)] - SUM[P_k * DF(m_k, g_k, i)]

Find i such that F(i) = 0.

The partial derivative of a discount factor with respect to i is:

  dDF/di = -DF(n,f,i) * [ f/(1+f*i) + n/(1+i) ]

So:
  F'(i) = SUM[A_j * dDF(n_j,f_j,i)/di] - SUM[P_k * dDF(m_k,g_k,i)/di]

Newton-Raphson iteration: i_{new} = i - F(i)/F'(i)

Iterate until the APR = i * w * 100 is stable to at least 2 decimal
places. Use all available precision throughout.

=========================================================================
9. SPECIAL FORMS (for reference)
=========================================================================
(a) Single advance A at t=0, n equal payments P, no odd first period:
    A = P * [(1 - (1+i)^-n) / i]

(b) Single advance A, n payments P, odd first fractional period f:
    A = P * [(1 - (1+i)^-n) / i] / (1 + f*i)

(c) Single advance, single payment (term <= 1 year):
    A = P / (1+i)  =>  i = P/A - 1  (direct solution)

(d) For transactions where a payment precedes the first advance:
    Use the date of the first cash flow event as the zero point.
    All advances and payments are discounted from this zero point.
    Payments at the zero point have DF = 1.

=========================================================================
10. TRANSACTION CLASSIFICATION (per Section 1026.22)
=========================================================================
REGULAR transaction: single advance, equal payment periods (measured
between consecutive payments), equal payment amounts.
  - An ODD FIRST PERIOD (consummation-to-first-payment differs from
    subsequent payment-to-payment intervals) does NOT make it irregular.
  - An ODD FIRST or FINAL PAYMENT (amount differs from middle payments)
    does NOT make it irregular.

IRREGULAR transaction: one that includes ANY of:
  - Multiple advances
  - Irregular payment PERIODS (consecutive payment intervals differ,
    beyond just the first period)
  - Irregular payment AMOUNTS (beyond just first/final payment)

=========================================================================
11. TOLERANCE RULES (Section 1026.22(a))
=========================================================================
Regular transactions: disclosed APR is accurate if within +/- 1/8 of 1
percentage point (0.125%) of the computed APR.

Irregular transactions: disclosed APR is accurate if within +/- 1/4 of 1
percentage point (0.250%) of the computed APR.

=========================================================================
12. REGULATORY WORKED EXAMPLES
=========================================================================
(a) Student Loan (Multiple Advance):
    Advances: $1800 on 9/5/78, 9/5/79, 9/5/80, 9/5/81;
              $1000 on 1/5/79, 1/5/80, 1/5/81, 1/5/82.
    Payments: 50 monthly payments of $240 starting 7/1/78.
    Zero point: 7/1/78 (first payment precedes first advance).
    Unit-period: 1 month, w = 12.
    From zero point to each $1800 advance: 2+4/30, 14+4/30, 26+4/30,
    38+4/30 unit-periods.
    From zero point to each $1000 advance: 6+4/30, 18+4/30, 30+4/30,
    42+4/30 unit-periods.
    APR = 32.04%.

(b) Step-Rate Loan (from Section 1026.22 commentary):
    $10,000 loan, 5 years. Monthly payments: $210.71 for years 1-2,
    $220.25 for years 3-4, $222.59 for year 5.
    APR = 10.75% (composite rate via IRR / discounted cash flow).
