================================================================================
MSRB RULE G-33: CALCULATIONS
Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board
================================================================================

SUMMARY: Prescribes standard formulas for the computation of accrued interest,
dollar price and yield; sets standards for accuracy; establishes day-counting
methods for municipal securities transactions.

================================================================================
SECTION (a): ACCRUED INTEREST
================================================================================

Accrued interest per $100 par value shall be computed as:

    AI = (R / M) * (A / E) * 100

where:
    R   = annual interest rate (decimal, e.g. 0.05 = 5%)
    M   = number of interest payment periods per year
    A   = number of accrued days from the beginning of the current interest
          payment period to the settlement date (per section (e))
    E   = number of days in the interest payment period in which the
          settlement date falls (per section (e))

For first coupon periods, the beginning of the period is the dated date
(issue date). Days are counted to INCLUDE the start date but NOT the
settlement date.

================================================================================
SECTION (b): INTEREST-BEARING SECURITIES
================================================================================

(b)(i) Dollar Price
-------------------

(b)(i)(A) Securities Paying Interest Solely at Redemption
---------------------------------------------------------
The dollar price per $100 par value:

    P = (RV + R * 100 * DIR / B) / (1 + Y * DSM / B)

where:
    DIR = number of days from issue (dated) date to redemption date (per (e))
    DSM = number of days from settlement date to maturity/redemption (per (e))
    B   = number of days in the year = 360 (per section (e))
    P   = dollar price per $100 par value
    R   = annual interest rate (decimal)
    RV  = redemption value per $100 par value
    Y   = yield (decimal)

Accrued interest for these securities:
    AI = R * 100 * DIS / B
where DIS = days from issue (dated) date to settlement date.

(b)(i)(B) Securities with Periodic Interest Payments
-----------------------------------------------------

(b)(i)(B)(1) -- One coupon period or less to redemption:

    P = (RV + C) / (1 + DSC/E * Y/M) - (A/E) * C

where:
    C   = periodic coupon payment = R / M * 100
    DSC = number of days from settlement date to next coupon date = E - A
    E   = number of days in the coupon period containing settlement
    A   = accrued days from period start to settlement date
    M   = coupon payments per year
    Y   = yield (decimal)
    RV  = redemption value per $100

(b)(i)(B)(2) -- More than one coupon period to redemption:

    P = RV / (1 + Y/M)^(N - 1 + DSC/E)
        + SUM(k=1 to N) [ C / (1 + Y/M)^(k - 1 + DSC/E) ]
        - (A/E) * C

where:
    N   = number of coupon payments from settlement to redemption (inclusive
          of payment on redemption date), expressed as a whole number
    DSC = E - A (days from settlement to next coupon)
    C   = R / M * 100
    k   = successively each whole number from 1 to N

For purposes of this formula, "^" denotes exponentiation, and "SUM"
denotes summation of the term for each value of k.

NOTE (2016 Amendment): The variable M represents the ACTUAL payment
frequency of the security (monthly=12, quarterly=4, semi-annual=2,
annual=1). Prior to February 23, 2016, semi-annual payments were assumed.

(b)(ii) Yield
-------------

(b)(ii)(A) Securities Paying Interest Solely at Redemption:

    Y = ((RV + R * 100 * DIR / B) / P - 1) * B / DSM

where all symbols are as defined in (b)(i)(A).

(b)(ii)(B) Securities with Periodic Interest Payments:

(b)(ii)(B)(1) -- One coupon period or less to redemption:

    Y = ((RV + C) / (P + (A/E) * C) - 1) * M * E / DSC

where all symbols are as defined in (b)(i)(B)(1).

(b)(ii)(B)(2) -- More than one coupon period to redemption:

The formula set forth in (b)(i)(B)(2) shall be used, solved iteratively
for Y given P. Since the formula cannot be algebraically inverted for
N > 1, numerical methods must be employed to find the yield Y that
produces the given dollar price P.

================================================================================
SECTION (c): DISCOUNTED SECURITIES
================================================================================

(c)(i) Dollar Price
-------------------

(c)(i)(A) Standard discounted securities:

    P = RV * (1 - DR * DSM / B)

where:
    DR  = discount rate (decimal)
    DSM = days from settlement to maturity (per section (e))
    B   = 360

(c)(ii) Return on Investment
----------------------------

(c)(ii)(A) Standard discounted securities:

    IR = ((RV - P) / P) * (B / DSM)

where IR = annualized return on investment (decimal).

================================================================================
SECTION (d): STANDARDS OF ACCURACY; TRUNCATION
================================================================================

(d)(i) Intermediate Values
All values used in computations of accrued interest, yield, and dollar price
shall be computed to not less than ten decimal places.

(d)(ii) Results of Computations
Results shall be presented as follows:

    (A) Accrued interest shall be TRUNCATED to three decimal places, and
        ROUNDED to two decimal places immediately prior to presentation.

    (B) Dollar prices shall be TRUNCATED to three decimal places
        immediately prior to presentation.

    (C) Yields shall be TRUNCATED to four decimal places, and ROUNDED
        to three decimal places.

ROUNDING CONVENTION: If the last digit after truncation is five or above,
the preceding digit shall be increased to the next highest number, and the
last digit shall be discarded.

================================================================================
SECTION (e): DAY COUNTING
================================================================================

(e)(i) Day Count Basis
Computations shall be made on the basis of a thirty-day month and a
three-hundred-sixty-day year (30/360).

(e)(ii) Day Count Formula (30/360)

    Number of Days = (Y2 - Y1) * 360 + (M2 - M1) * 30 + (D2 - D1)

where:
    Y1, M1, D1 = year, month, day of the period start date
    Y2, M2, D2 = year, month, day of the period end date

CRITICAL END-OF-MONTH ADJUSTMENTS (applied before the formula):
    Step 1: If D1 has a value of 31, change D1 to 30.
    Step 2: If D2 has a value of 31 AND the (adjusted) D1 has a value
            of 30 or 31, change D2 to 30.

Note: Step 2 depends on the result of Step 1. D2 is only adjusted to 30
if BOTH conditions hold: D2 equals 31 AND D1 (after Step 1) is >= 30.

TIME PERIOD CONVENTION: Time periods include the start date but do NOT
include the end date.

================================================================================
COUPON DATE GENERATION
================================================================================

For securities with periodic interest payments, coupon dates are generated
by stepping backward from the redemption date in intervals of (12/M) months.
For example, a semi-annual bond maturing January 1 has coupon dates on
January 1 and July 1 each year.

The number of remaining coupon payments N is the count of coupon dates
that fall strictly after the settlement date, up to and including the
redemption date.

The accrued days A are counted from the most recent coupon date on or
before the settlement date (or the dated date for the first coupon period)
to the settlement date. E is counted from that same start to the next
coupon date.

================================================================================
YIELD-TO-WORST / CALLABLE BOND TREATMENT
================================================================================

Per Rule G-15(a)(i)(A)(5)(c) and Rule G-12(c)(v)(I), securities shall be
priced to the LOWEST of:
    - price to call (for each call date/price in the schedule)
    - price to maturity

For yield-to-worst computation:
    - When computing PRICE from yield (compute=price): evaluate the
      G-33 price formula using each call date/price AND maturity as the
      redemption date/value. Select the LOWEST resulting dollar price.
    - When computing YIELD from price (compute=yield): evaluate the
      G-33 yield formula using each possible redemption. Select the
      LOWEST resulting yield.

The redemption date and value producing the worst (lowest) result are
reported as worst_date and worst_rv.

================================================================================
INTERPRETIVE GUIDANCE: DAY COUNTING EXAMPLE
================================================================================

QUESTION: A security dated June 15 paying interest on July 1 -- how many
days of accrued interest?

ANSWER: Applying the 30/360 formula:
    Y1=1982, M1=6, D1=15, Y2=1982, M2=7, D2=1
    Days = (0)*360 + (1)*30 + (1-15) = 30 + (-14) = 16 days.

The 15th of a month is the LAST day of the first half-month (days 1-15),
not the first day of the second half (days 16-30).

EXTENDED EXAMPLE: June 15 to September 1:
    16 days (Jun 15 to Jul 1) + 30 (July) + 30 (August) = 76 days.
