Implement `ReliableSender` and `ReliableReceiver` in `/app/transport.py` to deliver arbitrary byte sequences—including empty payloads and all 256 byte values (0x00–0xFF)—correctly and in order over the simulated lossy channel.

## Environment

- `/app/channel.py` — `LossyChannel` with configurable loss (up to 50%), delay, jitter, reordering, and bit-level corruption
- `/app/packet.py` — wire format with sequence numbers, ACK numbers, flags (DATA/ACK/FIN), advertised window, SACK blocks, and Internet checksum
- `/app/harness.py` — `transfer()` runs sender and receiver concurrently in separate threads
- `PYTHONPATH` includes both `/app` and `/opt/transport_lib`
- `gnuplot` is installed for data visualization

## Requirements

1. **Correct delivery**: byte-identical received data under packet loss from 0% to 50%, reordering, corruption, and adversarial combinations. Under zero-loss conditions, no retransmissions should occur.

2. **Sequence space**: must handle `seq_bits` from 8 to 16. With `seq_bits=8` the 256-entry space wraps frequently; correctness must hold across multiple full wraparounds including under packet loss.

3. **Adaptive sending rate**: the sender tracks a congestion window (`cwnd`). Under lossless conditions cwnd must reach at least 4; under lossy conditions cwnd must decrease at least once. The `cwnd_log` stat must contain at least 3 entries tracking these changes over time.

4. **Throughput**: at least 200 Kbps with `max_window=32`, zero loss, and ~10 ms base delay.

5. **Connection teardown**: sender transmits FIN after all data is acknowledged; receiver responds with FIN+ACK.

## Interface

```
ReliableSender(channel: LossyChannel, config: dict)
  .send(data: bytes) -> {"bytes_sent": int, "packets_sent": int,
                          "retransmissions": int,
                          "cwnd_log": [(elapsed_s, cwnd_val), ...]}

ReliableReceiver(channel: LossyChannel, config: dict)
  .receive() -> bytes
```

Config keys (defaults): `mss` (500), `max_window` (32), `seq_bits` (16), `initial_timeout_ms` (1000), `recv_window` (64).

## Visualization

Produce `/app/cwnd_evolution.svg` — a congestion-window-over-time plot generated by `gnuplot` from data captured during an actual protocol run. The x-axis must show elapsed time in seconds and the y-axis the cwnd value. The SVG must contain gnuplot's generator metadata.
