GROUND-COUPLED SLAB HEAT TRANSFER: 2D STEADY-STATE WITH HETEROGENEOUS MEDIA
=============================================================================

PHYSICAL SETUP:
A building's slab-on-grade floor loses heat to the surrounding ground.
The 2D cross-section uses left-right symmetry: only the right half is modeled
(x=0 at slab centerline).

The ground may consist of multiple horizontal soil layers with different thermal
conductivities, and may contain embedded insulation elements (rectangular regions
of low-conductivity material near the slab perimeter).

At the slab edge, a wall of finite thickness separates indoor and outdoor
environments. The temperature transitions linearly across the wall width
at the ground surface, preventing a boundary singularity.

COORDINATE SYSTEM:
- x: horizontal, x=0 at symmetry axis (slab center)
- z: vertical, z=0 at ground surface, increasing downward

DOMAIN: Rectangle [0, W] x [0, D]
  W = domain_width (half-domain width to far field)
  D = domain_depth (depth to deep ground boundary)

GOVERNING EQUATION:
  div(k(x,z) * grad(T)) = 0

  This is the variable-coefficient steady-state heat equation. The thermal
  conductivity k(x,z) varies spatially according to soil layers and any
  embedded insulation elements.

MATERIAL PROPERTIES:
  The conductivity field k(x,z) is defined by:

  1. Soil layers: horizontal strata specified as a list of
     {"depth_to": z_max, "conductivity": k_value}. Layer boundaries are
     horizontal lines at specified depths. The conductivity is constant
     within each layer.

  2. Insulation elements (optional): rectangular regions specified as
     {"x_start", "x_end", "z_start", "z_end", "conductivity"}.
     Where an insulation element exists, its conductivity overrides
     the soil layer conductivity.

  At interfaces between materials with different conductivities,
  both temperature and normal heat flux (k * dT/dn) must be continuous.
  The conductivity ratio across layer interfaces can be large (e.g. 47.5:1
  between soil and insulation).

BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:
  (Using normalized temperature theta = (T - T_outdoor) / (T_indoor - T_outdoor))

  Top surface (z=0):
    x in [0, B]           : theta = 1  (indoor temperature)
    x in (B, B+w]         : theta = (B + w - x) / w  (linear transition)
    x in (B+w, W]         : theta = 0  (outdoor temperature)

  Bottom (z=D)            : theta = 0
  Left  (x=0)            : d(theta)/dx = 0  (symmetry / adiabatic)
  Right (x=W)            : theta = 0

  B = slab_half_width
  w = wall_thickness

REQUIRED OUTPUTS:
  For each test case, compute three quantities:

  1. heat_loss_per_meter (Q): Total building-to-ground heat loss per unit
     slab length (W/m). Integrate the downward heat flux at z=0 over the
     slab+wall region [0, B+w], then multiply by 2 for symmetry:

       Q = 2 * integral_0^{B+w} [ -k(x,0) * delta_T * (d theta/dz)|_{z=0} ] dx

     where delta_T = T_indoor - T_outdoor.

  2. flux_density_avg (q_avg): Average slab heat flux density (W/m^2):
       q_avg = Q / (2 * B)

  3. psi_value: Linear thermal transmittance, also called the linear
     thermal bridge coefficient (W/(m*K)). This quantifies how much heat
     loss exceeds what a one-dimensional analysis would predict:

       psi = Q / delta_T  -  U_1d * (2 * B)

     where U_1d is the one-dimensional thermal transmittance through the
     soil column directly beneath the slab center:

       U_1d = 1 / sum(d_i / k_i)

     with d_i and k_i being the thickness and conductivity of each soil
     layer. NOTE: U_1d is computed from soil layers only; insulation
     elements (which are at the perimeter, not under the slab center)
     do not affect U_1d.

     The psi-value is always positive because 2D edge effects always
     add heat loss beyond the 1D prediction.
