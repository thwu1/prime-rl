# Internal Revenue Code § 121 — Exclusion of Gain from Sale of Principal Residence


## Computational Conventions

For the avoidance of ambiguity in implementation:

- **Two years.** Any reference to a "2-year period" in this section means a period of 730 days.
- **Five years.** The "5-year period ending on the date of sale" begins on the date obtained by subtracting 5 calendar years from the sale date (e.g., for a sale on January 15, 2025, the window begins January 15, 2020) and ends on but does not include the sale date itself.
- **Period measurement.** Periods of ownership and use are half-open intervals [begin, end). A period's length is (end − begin) measured in days. Where a period extends beyond the boundaries of the relevant window, only the portion falling within the window shall be counted.
- **Aggregation.** Where the statute refers to "periods aggregating" a duration, the total is the sum of the day-lengths of each applicable period after clipping to the relevant window.

---

## (a) Exclusion

Gross income shall not include gain realized on the sale or exchange of property if, during the 5-year period ending on the date of the sale or exchange, such property has been owned and used by the taxpayer as the taxpayer's principal residence for periods aggregating 2 years or more.

## (b) Limitations

### (1) In General

The amount of gain excluded from gross income under subsection (a) shall not exceed $250,000.

### (2) Special Rules for Joint Returns

In the case of a husband and wife who make a joint return for the taxable year of the sale or exchange:

**(A) $500,000 Limitation for Certain Joint Returns.** — Paragraph (1) shall be applied by substituting "$500,000" for "$250,000" if—

   (i) either spouse meets the ownership requirements of subsection (a) with respect to such property;

   (ii) both spouses meet the use requirements of subsection (a) with respect to such property; and

   (iii) neither spouse is ineligible for the benefits of subsection (a) with respect to such property by reason of paragraph (3) or subsection (d)(10).

**(B) Other Joint Returns.** — In the case of a joint return not described in subparagraph (A)—

   (i) the limitation under paragraph (1) shall be the sum of each spouse's applicable limitation amount, determined separately;

   (ii) for purposes of testing whether a spouse meets the ownership requirement of subsection (a), each spouse shall be treated as owning the property during any period of ownership by either spouse;

   (iii) a spouse's applicable limitation amount shall be—

   - $250,000, if such spouse meets the requirements of subsection (a) (with ownership determined under clause (ii)) and is not ineligible under paragraph (3) or subsection (d)(10); or

   - the reduced amount determined under subsection (c), if such spouse does not meet the requirements of subsection (a) but qualifies for a reduced exclusion under subsection (c) and is not ineligible under paragraph (3) or subsection (d)(10); or

   - $0, in all other cases.

### (3) Application to Only 1 Sale or Exchange Every 2 Years

Subsection (a) shall not apply to any sale or exchange by the taxpayer if, during the 2-year period ending on the date of such sale or exchange, there was any other sale or exchange by the taxpayer to which subsection (a) applied. The prior sale date falls within the 2-year window if the number of days from the prior sale date to the current sale date is 730 or fewer.

### (4) Special Rule for Certain Sales by Surviving Spouses

In the case of a sale or exchange of property by an unmarried individual whose spouse is deceased on the date of such sale or exchange, paragraph (1) shall be applied by substituting "$500,000" for "$250,000" if—

   (A) such sale or exchange occurs not later than 2 years (730 days) after the date of death of such spouse; and

   (B) as of immediately before the date of death, the requirements of paragraph (2)(A) would have been met with respect to such property, determined by evaluating each spouse's ownership and use of the property through the date of death using the 5-year window ending on the date of death, and testing ineligibility under paragraph (3) and subsection (d)(10) as of the date of death.

If the conditions of this paragraph are not met, the surviving spouse's exclusion shall be determined under paragraph (1) as a single taxpayer, evaluating the surviving spouse's own qualification through the sale date.

### (5) Exclusion of Gain Allocated to Nonqualified Use

**(A) In General.** — Subsection (a) shall not apply to so much of the gain from the sale or exchange of property as is allocable to periods of nonqualified use. The portion of gain allocable to nonqualified use is determined by multiplying the gain (as adjusted under subparagraph (D)) by the ratio that the aggregate periods of nonqualified use (as adjusted under subparagraph (C)) bear to the aggregate period of ownership of such property.

**(B) Period of Nonqualified Use.** — For purposes of subparagraph (A), the term "period of nonqualified use" means any period during which the property is not used as the principal residence of the taxpayer or the taxpayer's spouse, as specified in the taxpayer's declared nonqualified use periods.

**(C) Exceptions.** — For purposes of computing the nonqualified use ratio under subparagraph (A), the following shall not be taken into account as periods of nonqualified use:

   (i) any portion of a nonqualified use period that falls before the date on which the taxpayer first uses the property as a principal residence (determined from the earliest begin date across all principal-residence usage periods); and

   (ii) any portion of a nonqualified use period that falls after the date on which the taxpayer last uses the property as a principal residence (determined from the latest end date across all principal-residence usage periods).

**(D) Coordination with Recognition of Gain Attributable to Depreciation.** — For purposes of determining the gain subject to the nonqualified use allocation under subparagraph (A), the gain shall first be reduced (but not below zero) by the depreciation adjustments described in subsection (d)(6). The nonqualified use ratio is then applied to this reduced amount. Depreciation adjustments are separately accounted for as non-excludable gain under subsection (d)(6).

## (c) Exclusion for Taxpayers Failing to Meet Certain Requirements

### (1) In General

If a taxpayer fails to meet the ownership and use requirements of subsection (a) but has a qualifying reason described in paragraph (2), the dollar limitation under subsection (b)(1) shall be replaced by a reduced maximum amount. The reduced maximum amount bears the same ratio to the applicable limitation ($250,000, or $500,000 where applicable) as the shorter of—

   (i) the aggregate periods of ownership during the 5-year window, or

   (ii) the aggregate periods of use as a principal residence during the 5-year window

bears to 2 years.

### (2) Qualifying Reasons

A taxpayer has a qualifying reason for purposes of paragraph (1) if the sale or exchange is by reason of—

   (A) a change in place of employment,

   (B) health, or

   (C) unforeseen circumstances,

to the extent provided in regulations.

### (3) Interaction with Full Qualification

If the taxpayer meets the requirements of subsection (a) in full, the exclusion under subsection (a) applies with the full limitation under subsection (b)(1), regardless of whether a qualifying reason exists. Subsection (c) operates only as a fallback for taxpayers who do not meet subsection (a).

## (d) Special Rules

### (6) Recognition of Gain Attributable to Depreciation

The exclusion under subsection (a) shall not apply to so much of the gain from the sale of any property as does not exceed the depreciation adjustments (as defined in section 1250(b)(3)) attributable to the property. Such depreciation adjustments are always treated as non-excludable gain, distinct from and in addition to any gain rendered taxable by the limitations of this section.

### (10) Property Acquired in Like-Kind Exchange

In the case of a taxpayer who acquires property in an exchange to which section 1031 applies, subsection (a) shall not apply to the sale or exchange of such property if such sale or exchange occurs during the 5-year period beginning with the date of such acquisition. The 5-year period is determined by adding 5 calendar years to the acquisition date; the exclusion is unavailable if the sale date falls strictly before that boundary.

## Formal Compliance Invariants

Any compliant implementation of the exclusion computation must satisfy the following invariants. Each must be verified and the results recorded.

**[INVARIANT: single_cap_bound]** For any single-filing taxpayer with non-negative gain, non-negative depreciation not exceeding the gain, a nonqualified-use ratio in [0, 1], and a per-person cap in [0, 250000], the final excluded gain (after applying the cap, depreciation reduction, and nonqualified-use allocation) shall never exceed $250,000.

**[INVARIANT: joint_cap_bound]** For any joint-filing taxpayer pair with non-negative gain, non-negative depreciation not exceeding the gain, a nonqualified-use ratio in [0, 1], and a combined cap in [0, 500000], the final excluded gain shall never exceed $500,000.

**[INVARIANT: reduced_leq_full]** For any base cap ≥ 0 and aggregate ownership or use period in [0, 730) days, the reduced maximum exclusion computed under subsection (c)(1) shall never exceed the base cap.

**[INVARIANT: nq_use_can_reduce]** There exist values of gain > 0 and nonqualified-use ratio in (0, 1] such that applying the nonqualified-use allocation yields a strictly smaller excluded gain than would obtain without it.
