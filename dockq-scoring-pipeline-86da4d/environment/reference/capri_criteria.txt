CAPRI QUALITY CRITERIA FOR PROTEIN-PROTEIN DOCKING ASSESSMENT
================================================================================
Compiled from fetched web sources on 2026-07-06.

Primary sources:

     DOI: 10.1002/prot.10393 | PMID: 12784368

  2. Lensink MF, Méndez R, Wodak SJ. (2007) Proteins 69(4):704-18.
     DOI: 10.1002/prot.21804 | PMID: 17918726

The definitions below are extracted verbatim from the DockQ paper (Basu &
Wallner, 2016), which cites and formalizes the criteria originally established
by Méndez et al. (2003) and refined by Lensink, Méndez & Wodak (2007).

================================================================================
1. INTERFACE DEFINITION
================================================================================

The interface between the two interacting protein molecules (receptor and
ligand) is defined as any pair of heavy atoms from the two molecules within
5 Angstroms of each other.

================================================================================
2. QUALITY MEASURES
================================================================================

2a. fnat (Fraction of Native Contacts)
---------------------------------------
fnat is defined as the fraction of native interfacial contacts preserved in
the interface of the predicted complex.

  - "Native contacts" are residue-residue contacts across the interface in
    the experimentally determined (target/native) structure.
  - A contact is defined when any pair of heavy atoms from two residues on
    different chains are within 5 Angstroms.
  - fnat = (number of native contacts reproduced in the model) /
           (total number of native contacts in the target)
  - Range: [0, 1], where 1 = all native contacts reproduced perfectly.

2b. L-RMSD (Ligand RMSD, also written LRMS)
--------------------------------------------
LRMS is the Ligand Root Mean Square deviation calculated for the backbone
of the shorter chain (ligand) of the model after superposition of the longer
chain (receptor).

  - The receptor (longer chain) of the model is first superimposed onto
    the receptor of the target structure.
  - Then the RMSD of the backbone atoms of the ligand (shorter chain) is
    calculated WITHOUT further superposition.
  - This measures the overall positional accuracy of the ligand relative
    to the receptor.
  - Units: Angstroms. Lower is better.

2c. I-RMSD (Interface RMSD, also written iRMS)
-----------------------------------------------
For iRMS, the receptor-ligand interface in the target (native) is redefined
at a relatively relaxed atomic contact cutoff of 10 Angstroms (twice the
value used for fnat). The backbone atoms of these "interface" residues are
then superposed on their equivalents in the predicted complex (model) to
compute the iRMS.

  - Interface residues are identified using a 10 Angstrom heavy-atom
    distance cutoff between chains in the native structure.
  - The backbone atoms of these interface residues in the model are
    superimposed onto their counterparts in the native structure.
  - iRMS is the resulting RMSD after this superposition.
  - This measures the local accuracy of the interface geometry.
  - Units: Angstroms. Lower is better.

================================================================================
3. CAPRI QUALITY CATEGORIES
================================================================================

The CAPRI evaluation uses different cutoffs on fnat, L-RMSD, and I-RMSD to
assign predicted docking models into four quality classes. The criteria below
are extracted directly from Basu & Wallner (2016), which formalizes the
classification from Lensink, Méndez & Wodak (2007):

  HIGH QUALITY:
    fnat >= 0.5  AND  (L-RMSD <= 1.0  OR  I-RMSD <= 1.0)

  MEDIUM QUALITY:
    ( (fnat >= 0.3 AND fnat < 0.5) AND (L-RMSD <= 5.0 OR I-RMSD <= 2.0) )
    OR
    ( fnat >= 0.5 AND L-RMSD > 1.0 AND I-RMSD > 1.0 )

  ACCEPTABLE QUALITY:
    ( (fnat >= 0.1 AND fnat < 0.3) AND (L-RMSD <= 10.0 OR I-RMSD <= 4.0) )
    OR
    ( fnat >= 0.3 AND L-RMSD > 5.0 AND I-RMSD > 2.0 )

  INCORRECT:
    fnat < 0.1
    OR
    (L-RMSD > 10.0 AND I-RMSD > 4.0)

================================================================================
4. SUMMARY TABLE
================================================================================

  Category    | fnat       | L-RMSD (A) | I-RMSD (A)
  ------------|------------|------------|------------
  High        | >= 0.5     | <= 1.0     | <= 1.0
  Medium      | >= 0.3     | <= 5.0     | <= 2.0
  Acceptable  | >= 0.1     | <= 10.0    | <= 4.0
  Incorrect   | < 0.1      | > 10.0     | > 4.0

NOTE: The classification uses a hierarchical scheme where fnat is checked
first, then L-RMSD OR I-RMSD must satisfy the threshold (i.e., meeting
EITHER the L-RMSD or I-RMSD criterion is sufficient). Categories are
assigned top-down: High first, then Medium, then Acceptable, else Incorrect.

================================================================================
5. KEY NOTES
================================================================================

- The criteria were first introduced in Méndez et al. (2003) and refined
  in Lensink, Méndez & Wodak (2007).

- fnat uses a 5A heavy-atom contact cutoff for defining interface contacts.

- I-RMSD uses a 10A heavy-atom contact cutoff for defining interface residues
  (more relaxed than fnat).

- L-RMSD measures global ligand placement; I-RMSD measures local interface
  accuracy. A model can have poor L-RMSD but still acceptable I-RMSD.

- The DockQ score (Basu & Wallner, 2016) combines all three measures into a
  single continuous score [0,1]:
    DockQ = (fnat + RMS_scaled(L-RMSD, 8.5) + RMS_scaled(I-RMSD, 1.5)) / 3
  where RMS_scaled(RMS, d) = 1 / (1 + (RMS/d)^2)

- Approximate DockQ thresholds corresponding to CAPRI categories:
    Incorrect:  DockQ < 0.23
    Acceptable: 0.23 <= DockQ < 0.49
    Medium:     0.49 <= DockQ < 0.80
    High:       DockQ >= 0.80
