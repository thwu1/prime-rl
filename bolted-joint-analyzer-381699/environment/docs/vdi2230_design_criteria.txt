Design Criteria and Preload Requirements (VDI 2230)
====================================================


1. Embedding Loss
-----------------
Embedding is the permanent plastic deformation (flattening of asperities)
that occurs at all contact surfaces when a bolted joint is first loaded.
This deformation results in a loss of bolt preload because the effective
bolt stretch is reduced.

The per-interface embedding displacement depends on the surface finish
quality of the clamped parts:

  Surface class     Embedding per interface
  -------------------------------------------
  Fine              2 um (micrometres)
  Normal            3 um
  Rough             5 um

Total embedding displacement:
  f_Z = embedding_per_interface * number_of_interfaces

The preload force lost to embedding is calculated by multiplying the
total embedding displacement by the series spring rate of the bolt-joint
system:

  F_embedding = f_Z * K_series

where K_series = (K_b * K_j) / (K_b + K_j)

Note: f_Z must be in mm (divide um by 1000) when K_series is in N/mm.

The number of interfaces counts all mating surfaces in the joint stack.
For example, a joint with two clamped plates has 3 interfaces: bolt-head
to plate 1, plate 1 to plate 2, and plate 2 to nut.


2. Shear Grip Requirement
--------------------------
For friction-grip (slip-critical) joints, the bolt preload must provide
sufficient normal force to resist the applied shear through friction at
the interfaces. The minimum clamp force required is:

  F_shear = V / mu_eff

where:
  V      = applied shear force
  mu_eff = effective friction coefficient at the interfaces

If a friction shim is present between the clamped surfaces, use the
friction shim's coefficient instead of the bare interface friction
coefficient. Friction shims typically have much higher friction
coefficients (0.5-0.7) compared to bare steel (0.10-0.20), dramatically
reducing the clamp force requirement.


3. Axial Load Clamp Reduction
------------------------------
When an external tensile (axial) force is applied to the joint, it
partially relieves the clamping force. The amount of clamp reduction
depends on the load factor phi:

  F_axial_reduction = (1 - phi) * F_axial

This is the portion of the external axial load that reduces the clamp
force rather than adding to bolt tension.


4. Total Preload Requirement
-----------------------------
The minimum preload that the bolt must maintain after all losses and
external loads is the sum of:

  F_required = F_embedding + F_shear + F_axial_reduction

The bolt's minimum achievable preload (at maximum friction) must exceed
this requirement for the joint to be adequate.


5. Design Margin
----------------
The design margin quantifies how much safety the joint provides:

  DM = F_min_preload / F_required

  DM >= 1.0: Joint is adequate (preload exceeds requirement)
  DM < 1.0:  Joint is inadequate (preload may be insufficient)

In practice, DM values of 1.2-1.5 or higher are preferred to account
for uncertainties not captured in the deterministic analysis.


6. Nut Compatibility (ISO 898-2 / DIN 934)
-------------------------------------------
The nut must be strong enough that the thread does not strip before the
bolt reaches its full tensile capacity. The check is:

  Nut proof load >= A_s * UTS_bolt

where A_s is the thread stress area and UTS_bolt is the bolt's ultimate
tensile strength.

IMPORTANT: DIN standard nuts have approximately 10% lower proof loads
than ISO equivalents of the same class marking. A DIN class 8 nut may
be marginal or incompatible with an 8.8 bolt, while the ISO equivalent
is fully compatible. Always use the correct proof load table for the
specified nut standard.


7. Washer Suitability (ISO 898-3)
----------------------------------
High-strength bolts require hardened washers to prevent bearing surface
damage. Minimum washer hardness requirements by bolt property class:

  Property class     Min washer hardness
  ----------------------------------------
  Below 8.8          No specific requirement
  8.8                200 HV
  10.9               300 HV
  12.9               380 HV

If the bolt property class is 8.8 or above and NO washer is present,
the joint configuration is unsuitable regardless of hardness value.

For grades below 8.8, washers are optional and always considered suitable.


8. Temperature Suitability
---------------------------
Each bolt material has a maximum recommended service temperature beyond
which mechanical properties degrade unacceptably:

  Material           Max temperature (deg C)
  -------------------------------------------
  ISO 898            150
  A193 B7            400
  A193 B16           500
  A286               700
  Inconel 718        760

The temperature check is simply: operating temperature <= material limit.


9. Thread Root Radius (ISO 68-1)
---------------------------------
For ISO metric threads, the permissible thread root radius is bounded by:

  r_min = 0.125 * p
  r_max = 0.144 * p

where p is the thread pitch. This range ensures adequate fatigue life
while maintaining proper thread engagement.


10. Differential Thermal Expansion
------------------------------------
When the bolt and clamped joint materials have different coefficients of
thermal expansion (CTE), temperature changes from the assembly temperature
alter the preload. The assembly reference temperature is 20 C.

The differential thermal load is:

  F_thermal = (alpha_joint - alpha_bolt) * delta_T * L_grip * K_series

where:
  alpha_joint = CTE of the joint (clamped) material in ppm/K (= 1e-6/K)
  alpha_bolt  = CTE of the bolt material in ppm/K
  delta_T     = T_operating - T_assembly = T_operating - 20 (in K or C)
  L_grip      = grip length in mm
  K_series    = series spring rate = (K_b * K_j) / (K_b + K_j) in N/mm

Note: multiply alpha values by 1e-6 to convert from ppm/K to 1/K before
the multiplication. F_thermal result is in Newtons.

Physical interpretation:
  - Positive F_thermal (alpha_joint > alpha_bolt): the joint material
    expands more than the bolt, increasing bolt stretch and preload.
  - Negative F_thermal (alpha_bolt > alpha_joint): the bolt expands more,
    reducing bolt stretch and decreasing preload. This is dangerous.

When F_thermal is negative, the magnitude of this loss must be included
in the total preload requirement:

  F_required = F_embedding + F_shear + F_axial_reduction + max(0, -F_thermal)

When F_thermal is positive (preload gain), it does NOT reduce the
preload requirement — it provides additional margin but the base
requirement remains unchanged.

Typical CTE values (ppm/K) for bolt materials:

  Material           CTE (ppm/K)
  --------------------------------
  ISO 898            12.0
  A193 B7            12.0
  A193 B16           16.5
  A286               16.0
  Inconel 718        13.0

The joint material CTE is specified in the input specification
(cte_ppm_per_K field in the [joint] section).
