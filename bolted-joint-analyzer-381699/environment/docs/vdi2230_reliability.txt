Reliability Analysis - Stress-Strength Interference Method
==========================================================


1. Motivation
-------------
The deterministic design margin (DM = F_min / F_required) provides a
single-point assessment of joint adequacy. However, both the achievable
preload and the preload requirement are subject to statistical variation.
The stress-strength interference method provides a probabilistic
assessment of joint reliability.


2. Statistical Model
--------------------
Model the achievable bolt preload as a normal distribution:

  F_preload ~ Normal(mu_p, sigma_p)

where:
  mu_p    = (F_max + F_min) / 2       (mean of preload range)
  sigma_p = (F_max - F_min) / 6       (treating min/max as +/- 3 sigma)

The rationale for sigma_p: the minimum and maximum preload values from
the torque-tension equation at extreme friction represent approximately
the 99.7% confidence interval (3 sigma on each side of the mean).

Model the preload requirement as a normal distribution:

  F_required ~ Normal(mu_r, sigma_r)

where:
  mu_r    = total_preload_requirement   (deterministic value)
  sigma_r = 0.10 * total_preload_requirement   (10% coefficient of
            variation, accounting for uncertainty in loads, embedding,
            and friction coefficients)


3. Probability of Failure
-------------------------
Failure occurs when the preload requirement exceeds the available preload:

  P(failure) = P(F_required > F_preload)

For two independent normal distributions, the difference
Z = F_preload - F_required is also normally distributed:

  Z ~ Normal(mu_p - mu_r, sqrt(sigma_p^2 + sigma_r^2))

The probability of failure is:

  P(failure) = P(Z < 0) = Phi( -(mu_p - mu_r) / sqrt(sigma_p^2 + sigma_r^2) )

where Phi is the standard normal cumulative distribution function (CDF).

The standard normal CDF can be computed as:

  Phi(x) = 0.5 * erfc(-x / sqrt(2))

where erfc is the complementary error function.


4. Interpretation
-----------------
Typical target failure probabilities for bolted joints:

  Application                   Target P(failure)
  -------------------------------------------------
  Non-critical structural       < 1e-3  (0.1%)
  Safety-critical               < 1e-5  (0.001%)
  Aerospace/nuclear             < 1e-7  (0.00001%)

A joint with DM >> 1 will have P(failure) approaching zero.
A joint with DM near 1.0 will have P(failure) in the range 0.01-0.10
depending on the scatter in preload and requirement distributions.
A joint with DM < 1.0 will have P(failure) > 0.5 (more likely to fail
than not).
