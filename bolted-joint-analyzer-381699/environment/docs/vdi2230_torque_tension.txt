VDI 2230 Torque-Tension Analysis
=================================


1. Overview
-----------
When a bolt is tightened to a specified torque T (Nm), the resulting axial
preload F (N) depends on the geometric and frictional properties of the
threaded connection. The VDI 2230 methodology uses the "long-form"
torque-tension equation that separates the three torque-absorbing components:

  T = F * [ p/(2*pi) + mu_t * d2/(2*cos(alpha)) + mu_b * D_km/2 ]

where:
  p       = thread pitch (mm), from thread geometry tables
  d2      = pitch diameter (mm), from thread geometry tables
  alpha   = thread flank half-angle; for ISO metric threads alpha = 30 deg
  mu_t    = coefficient of friction in the thread contact zone
  mu_b    = coefficient of friction at the bearing surface (under the head
            or nut)
  D_km    = mean bearing diameter (mm)

Note: all length dimensions in the bracket are in millimetres. Since T is
in Nm, conversion requires multiplying the bracket by 1e-3 (mm to m):

  T = F * factor_mm * 1e-3

  => F = T / (factor_mm * 1e-3)

2. Mean Bearing Diameter
------------------------
The mean bearing diameter D_km is the arithmetic average of the clearance
hole diameter and the bearing contact outer diameter:

  D_km = (d_hole + d_bearing) / 2

Standard values:
  d_hole    = d + 1 mm    (standard metric clearance hole, medium fit)
  d_bearing = 1.5 * d     (standard hex head/nut bearing diameter)

where d is the nominal bolt diameter in mm.

3. Friction Scatter and Preload Range
-------------------------------------
In practice, friction coefficients are never precisely known. They vary
due to surface finish, lubrication, plating, contamination, and tightening
speed. The VDI 2230 approach evaluates the torque-tension equation at both
extremes of the friction range:

  - Maximum preload (F_max): occurs at MINIMUM friction coefficients
    (both mu_t_min and mu_b_min), because less torque is absorbed by
    friction, so more goes into stretching the bolt.

  - Minimum preload (F_min): occurs at MAXIMUM friction coefficients
    (both mu_t_max and mu_b_max), because more torque is absorbed by
    friction, leaving less for bolt stretch.

The ratio F_max/F_min is the "tightening factor" or "scatter factor",
typically ranging from 1.4 to 2.5 depending on the tightening method
and friction control.

4. Practical Friction Ranges
----------------------------
Typical thread and bearing friction coefficient ranges for steel fasteners:

  Condition                    mu_t          mu_b
  --------------------------------------------------
  Dry/as-received              0.12-0.18     0.12-0.18
  Oiled                        0.10-0.16     0.10-0.16
  MoS2 paste                   0.06-0.10     0.06-0.10
  Cadmium plated               0.08-0.12     0.08-0.14
  Zinc flake coated            0.09-0.14     0.10-0.16
  PTFE-coated                  0.04-0.08     0.04-0.08

Note: thread and bearing friction may differ significantly. It is common
for bearing friction to be different from thread friction due to different
surface conditions and contact pressures.
