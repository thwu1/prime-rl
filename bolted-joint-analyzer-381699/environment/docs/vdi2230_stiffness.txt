Bolt and Joint Stiffness Models (VDI 2230)
==========================================


1. Bolt Stiffness - Compliance Summation Method
------------------------------------------------
The VDI 2230 methodology models the bolt as several cylindrical sections
connected in series, each contributing compliance (the inverse of stiffness).
The total bolt compliance is the sum of the individual section compliances.

For a standard hex bolt in a through-bolted joint, the three relevant
sections are:

  Section 1 - Head engagement zone:
    Length:         L_head = 0.4 * d
    Cross-section:  A_head = shank cross-sectional area = pi/4 * d^2

  Section 2 - Grip (clamped) zone:
    Length:         L_grip = grip_length
    Cross-section:  A_grip = thread stress area A_s

  Section 3 - Nut engagement zone:
    Length:         L_nut = 0.4 * d
    Cross-section:  A_nut = thread stress area A_s

Each section's compliance is: delta_i = L_i / (E * A_i)

Total bolt compliance: delta_b = delta_head + delta_grip + delta_nut

Bolt stiffness: K_b = 1 / delta_b   (units: N/mm)

Use E = 210,000 MPa (210 GPa) for steel bolts (applies to all ISO 898
property classes and most alloy steel grades).

Note: The head and nut engagement zones use 0.4*d as an empirical
approximation validated by FEA studies. The head section uses the full
shank area because it represents the unthreaded portion beneath the head,
while the grip and nut sections use the stress area because the thread
reduces the effective cross-section.


2. Joint Stiffness - Roetscher Pressure-Cone (Frustum) Model
-------------------------------------------------------------
The VDI 2230 standard models the clamped material as a pair of truncated
cones (frustums) that define the volume of compressed material. For a
symmetric through-bolted joint (where the loading plane is at the
midpoint of the grip), the joint consists of two identical frustums
connected in series.

Geometric parameters:
  - Each frustum has height h = grip_length / 2 (half the total grip)
  - Pressure cone half-angle: alpha = 30 degrees
  - Inner diameter (clearance hole): d_h = d + 1 mm
  - Bearing diameter (washer/head contact): d_w = 1.5 * d
  - Outer diameter at the midplane:
      D_A = d_w + grip_length * tan(alpha)
    This represents the cone expanding from d_w at the bearing face to
    D_A at the joint midplane, spanning height = grip_length/2, so:
      D_A = d_w + 2 * (grip_length/2) * tan(alpha) = d_w + grip_length * tan(alpha)

Single frustum stiffness (derived from integration of the conical
compression region):

  K_f = (E_joint * pi * d_h * tan(alpha)) / ln( ((D_A - d_h)*(d_w + d_h)) / ((D_A + d_h)*(d_w - d_h)) )

where E_joint is the elastic modulus of the clamped material in MPa.

Two frustums in series:
  K_j = K_f / 2   (since 1/K_j = 1/K_f + 1/K_f = 2/K_f)

This result follows from the standard series-spring relation.


3. Load Introduction Factor (Force Ratio)
------------------------------------------
The load factor phi quantifies how an external axial force is shared
between additional bolt tension and relief of the clamp force:

  phi = K_b / (K_b + K_j)

For a typical bolted joint, phi ranges from 0.05 to 0.25. Lower values
are better because they mean less of the external load reaches the bolt.

Physical interpretation:
  - If phi = 0.15, then 15% of any external axial load adds to bolt
    tension, while 85% reduces the clamp force.
  - Stiffer joints (higher K_j) relative to bolt stiffness (K_b)
    produce lower phi, which is desirable.

4. Series Spring Rate
---------------------
The combined bolt-joint series spring rate is used for embedding loss
calculations:

  K_series = (K_b * K_j) / (K_b + K_j)

This is the standard formula for two springs in series.
