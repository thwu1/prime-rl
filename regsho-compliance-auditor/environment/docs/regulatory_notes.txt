FINRA Regulatory Reference Notes
=================================

1. FINRA Regulatory Notice 12-38, FAQ Q7 — Fractional Share Reporting
---------------------------------------------------------------------
"If a firm has a fractional short-interest position (e.g., 125.6 shares),
it should truncate the position to reflect a whole number when reporting
such positions to FINRA pursuant to FINRA Rule 4560, instead of rounding
the position up or down. For example, firms should report short-interest
of 125.6 shares in XYZ as 125 shares."


2. FINRA Consolidated Short Interest — Derived Field Notes
-----------------------------------------------------------
The following fields in consolidated short interest data are derived from
primary reported values and must be independently verifiable:

- changePreviousNumber: the arithmetic difference between the current and
  previous reporting period short positions.

- changePercent: the percentage change in short position from the previous
  reporting period. Not applicable when the previous period position is zero.

- daysToCoverQuantity: a coverage ratio representing the relationship between
  the current short position and average daily trading volume. This field is
  bounded: FINRA systems cap the published value at 999.99 for securities
  where the ratio would exceed this threshold (including securities with no
  meaningful trading volume). For securities where the ratio is positive but
  less than one full day of coverage, the published value is 1.00.


3. SEC Regulation SHO, Rule 203(b)(3) — Threshold Securities
--------------------------------------------------------------
A "threshold security" is an equity security for which there is an aggregate
fail-to-deliver position at a registered clearing agency of:
  (i) 10,000 shares or more per security, AND
  (ii) equal to at least 0.5% of the issuer's total shares outstanding.

Securities meeting these criteria are placed on the threshold list with
regShoThresholdFlag=Y.

Under Rule 203(b)(3), if a security has had such fails for five consecutive
settlement days, a participant that has a fail-to-deliver position in that
security must close out the position by purchasing securities of like kind
and quantity. The close-out obligation is triggered when the security has
appeared on the threshold list for 5 consecutive settlement days.

A gap in appearance on the threshold list resets the consecutive-day counter.

FINRA Rule 4320 provides separate obligations for certain OTC equity
securities and may apply to securities independently of Reg SHO thresholds.
Securities flagged only under Rule 4320 (rule4320Flag=Y, regShoThresholdFlag=N)
are tracked separately.
