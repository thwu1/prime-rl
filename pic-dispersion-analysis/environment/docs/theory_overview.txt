WarpX Theory Overview
=====================

WarpX implements the Particle-In-Cell (PIC) method for kinetic plasma
simulations. The standard electromagnetic PIC algorithm proceeds as:

1. Interpolate fields (E, B) from grid to particle positions
2. Push particles using the Lorentz force equation
3. Deposit charge and current densities from particles to grid
4. Advance electromagnetic fields on the grid

Field Solvers
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The default electromagnetic solver in WarpX is the finite-difference
time-domain (FDTD) method on a staggered Yee grid. Alternative solvers
include the pseudo-spectral analytical time-domain (PSATD) method.

The FDTD method discretizes Maxwell's equations on a staggered grid
where electric and magnetic field components are offset by half a cell
in space and half a time step in time. The Yee scheme has specific
implications for numerical dispersion and stability.

Numerical Properties
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

FDTD methods introduce numerical artifacts compared to exact solutions:

- Modified dispersion relation: numerical phase and group velocities
  differ from the speed of light in vacuum
- Numerical Cherenkov radiation
- Grid heating from aliased modes

The time step is constrained by a stability condition that depends on
the grid cell sizes and the speed of light. Violation of this condition
leads to exponentially growing, unphysical field amplitudes.

Resolution Requirements
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Adequate resolution in PIC simulations requires sufficient cells to
resolve key physical length scales of the problem:

- The laser wavelength (for laser-driven simulations)
- The plasma skin depth
- The plasma wavelength

Under-resolved simulations produce unphysical artifacts including
numerical heating, spurious self-injection, and incorrect wake
structure. The required resolution depends on the specific physics
being studied and the acceptable level of numerical error.

Laser-Plasma Acceleration
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In laser-plasma acceleration (LPA), an intense laser pulse drives
plasma wakefields that can trap and accelerate electrons to high
energies. Key physical parameters include:

- The laser's normalized vector potential (a_0)
- The ratio of plasma density to critical density
- The plasma wavelength, which sets the accelerating structure size
- The laser Rayleigh length, which limits the interaction distance

The electromagnetic wave can only propagate through plasma when the
electron density is below the critical density for the given laser
frequency.
